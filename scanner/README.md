# Scanner

The embedded scanner opens `live-camera.js` immediately. It requests a rear camera without audio, analyses frames at a maximum edge of 480 pixels, and draws observed curved yellow borders and calibration strips over an uncropped video preview. `processing/liveDetection.js` also uses the paper perimeter to support faint or broken rails. Spatial regularization removes small false kinks while preserving broad curvature. Drawing runs on animation frames, with a common motion filter for the outline and markers. Capture decisions use unfiltered measurements. Brief gaps retain the outline without counting missing observations towards capture; stale observations expire after 350 milliseconds.

Automatic capture requires a complete, stable frame across at least five distinct qualified video frames and 800 milliseconds, sufficient confidence, exposure and sharpness. Recent sharp frames are buffered before capture and ranked with the shutter burst; three alternatives are retained for review. The original photo is visible immediately while detailed detection runs in `processing/detection-worker.js`. Surface resampling and colour correction yield to the browser, and changing the selected frame cancels obsolete work. A failed correction leaves a clearly labelled original photo available.

The user confirms before the existing session-checked scanner bridge inserts a decoded image. Originals stay in memory until close or retake; no video recording or upload is used for detection. Still detection revalidates the live curved contours against the captured pixels and compares coarse and fine reconstructions against the same source image. Invalid geometry leaves an explicitly labelled original instead of accepting an unrelated background crop.

Curved-page correction uses printed boundaries and calibration rails to estimate a smooth surface. `surface-parameterization.js` measures the five-millimetre calibration cells, fixes their numbering from the coloured markers, and follows camera rulings to correct horizontal foreshortening. When the printed dot grid provides enough observations, `metric-surface.js` fits a physical 210 × 297 mm developable surface and a pinhole camera. It preserves arc length, estimates the bend direction and depth profile, and constrains the fit with the observed borders. Most grid points are withheld from fitting to validate the resulting projection. Ambiguous grid numbering, inconsistent residuals and folded projections reject the 3D fit. Insufficient grid evidence retains the boundary-based correction; neither model can recover text hidden behind a fold.

Camera access requires HTTPS (or localhost) and user permission. The parent iframe grants `allow="camera"`. Android WebView additionally needs a signed app declaring `android.permission.CAMERA`; older APKs without it can still import a photo. Extremely faded or occluded frames may require manual capture and review.

Run `npm run test:unit` and `npx playwright test tests/e2e/live-scanner.spec.mjs tests/e2e/scanner-*.spec.mjs` for detector, camera lifecycle, insertion, cancellation, image quality and perspective regressions. Camera tests use real Canvas MediaStreams with synthetic printed-page footage. Independent surface fixtures project cylindrical and S-shaped paper through a pinhole camera and measure interior writing. Regression scenes include pale books, yellow distractors, faint printing, subpixel shifts and camera JPEG capture. Physical-device testing remains useful for autofocus and lighting differences.
